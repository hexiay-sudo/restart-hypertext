I DIED.

At twenty-two, I decided I could not carry another tomorrow.

I was an Informatics student in Seattle. Five quarters left. Too many deadlines. Two internships on my résumé, and a quiet fear that I knew nothing. I kept telling my family I was doing fine.

That night, the pressure felt bigger than the room. I tried to end my life. I will not describe how. I remember being afraid. I remember thinking of my little sister, still young enough to believe I knew what I was doing.

I had wanted everything to stop.
Then the world went silent.

DEATH IS QUIET.

Not peaceful. Just quiet.

I try to breathe. I cannot tell if I have lungs. I try to check my phone. I cannot find my hands.

My first thought is not about God. It is: Will my parents see my unfinished assignments?

Somewhere in the dark, a printer starts working. It prints one sheet of paper.

“Congratulations,” a voice says. “You have reached the end.”

PLEASE WAIT.

I am in a white office with no windows. The lights hum like tired insects. A clock has thirteen hands. None of them move.

Behind the desk is a clerk wearing a name tag that says NOBODY.

“You asked to leave your life,” he says. “We have approved your request.”

“Am I really dead?”

“That information is not available to customers.”

He gives me three forms. I can live without failure, without goodbyes, or without time running out.

Under each form, in tiny letters: Every wish has a cost.

100%.

I graduate on time. Every line of Python works. My résumé looks impressive. Even the Seattle rain waits until I am indoors.

At graduation, everyone claps at the same time. The professor gives me two envelopes. Both have my handwriting.

KNOW EVERY ANSWER
or
NEVER DISAPPOINT ANYONE

The crowd is still clapping. Nobody is breathing.

STAY.

I am home. No long flights. No airport goodbyes. My little sister is laughing with her friends over hot pot.

On the table are two tickets. Neither has a date.

KEEP THIS MOMENT
or
BRING EVERYONE HOME

The soup is boiling. I cannot see the fire.

∞

I have unlimited time. No graduation deadline. I can learn Python next week. I can call home tomorrow. I can become brave next year.

A clock offers me two choices.

LIVE FOREVER
or
REPEAT ONE PERFECT DAY

Both hands of the clock point at me.

ANSWER KEY.

I know every answer. Every exam, every interview, every word my parents will say before they say it.

At dinner, my sister laughs. I already know the joke. I even know the day she will stop laughing.

I try to ask a question I cannot answer. The room goes silent. On the wall: NO FURTHER QUESTIONS.

For the first time, I want to be wrong.

GOOD BOY.

Every professor praises me. Every employer hires me. My parents smile whenever they see me. My LinkedIn becomes a museum about myself.

Then I notice their smiles never move. My phone sends I'M PROUD OF YOU every minute. The professor keeps applauding while I sleep.

I look in the mirror. My face is smiling. I am not. A note says: DISAPPOINTMENT REMOVED. CHOICE REMOVED.

I whisper something imperfect.

AGAIN.

My sister is laughing over hot pot. I want to protect this small happy moment. The world agrees.

Tomorrow, the soup boils the same way. The same chopsticks fall. Everyone laughs at the same second. Again. Again.

Years pass outside. Inside, she never gets older. I ask what she wants to become. She says, 'What do you mean, become?'

I open the door and say goodbye.

ARRIVALS ONLY.

Everyone I love comes home. No more distance between Shanghai and Seattle. No more airports where I have to wave goodbye.

Our dinner table fills up. Then more chairs arrive. Old friends. Classmates. People I barely remember. The hallway gets longer every night.

My sister asks to go outside. The door opens into another dining room. At the airport, every screen says WELCOME BACK. There is no departure gate.

I look for an exit.

YEAR 10,000.

I never age. I finish my degree. I try every job and visit every city. Eventually I run out of first times.

The university is gone. The streets have new names. My childhood photos show faces whose voices I no longer remember.

I still have my mother's number. Nobody answers. A clock asks: HOW MUCH MORE TIME DO YOU NEED?

I ask for an ending.

THURSDAY.

It is a perfect day. I finish my homework early. My parents call. My sister tells me a funny story. I fall asleep happy.

Morning comes. Thursday again. Same message. Same sunlight. Same joke. After a hundred Thursdays, I try to tell my sister what is happening.

She says the same first sentence. I write HELP on my arm. The next morning, my skin is clean.

For the first time, I want Friday.

RETURN DENIED.

“You came back,” the clerk says. “Most people think the second choice will save them.”

“Every world looked beautiful from the outside,” I say. “Then I had to live in it.”

He opens my file. Under REASON FOR DEPARTURE, someone has typed: TOO MUCH TO LIVE FOR, TOO AFRAID TO LIVE IT.

“I was scared of failing, of leaving home, of time running out. I thought dying would end the questions.”

“And now?”

“Now I'm scared I won't get to try again.”

The clerk looks up for the first time. A sound comes through the ceiling. Beep. Beep. Beep.

“That's strange,” he whispers. “The dead don't have a heartbeat.”

NOT DEAD.

White ceiling. A hospital bed. A monitor beside me. My throat hurts. My hands are real.

Someone tells me I survived. For a moment, I cannot speak. Then I cry. I feel the blanket, the cold water, the air entering my lungs. I had been afraid of waking up to the same problems. Now I am grateful that I can.

My phone is on the table. There are missed calls from home. I think about my parents, my little sister, the next flight, the next ordinary dinner.

I still have deadlines. I still do not know what I will do after graduation. I will still make mistakes. I will need help.

Morning light comes through the window. Outside, someone is late for work. A bus stops. The world is moving forward. I never thought I would be so happy to see an ordinary Thursday.

I wanted a new life. I got this one back. It is messy, uncertain, and still mine.

For once, that is enough.
